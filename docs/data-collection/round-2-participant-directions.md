Introduction:

In order to evaluate the relative benefits of various data for velocity-prediction-based energy management (PEM) in hybrid electric vehicles (HEV), a real-world dataset will be generated for urban conditions by driving through the city of Fort Collins Colorado. The test will generate the following data: autonomous driver assistance system (ADAS) data for the vehicle forward cone from a smart camera and smart radar, which is a common setup for production vehicle ADAS systems, Simulated vehicle to vehicle (V2V) data from instrumented vehicle being driven in the vicinity of the ego vehicle, and vehicle to infrastructure (V2I) data in the form of traffic signal information and segment travel times. The ego vehicle will be a specially instrumented research vehicle with the afore-mentioned ADAS sensors and a stereo camera, while the V2V data will be generated by students driving personal vehicles with a Freematics GPS/IMU/OBD logger inserted into their vehicles’ OBD2 port.

Roles:

1.  V2V Driver: Participant driving an instrumented personal vehicle to simulate V2V data.

2.  Ego Driver: Driver of the Malibu Mule vehicle.

3.  ADAS System Operator: Passenger in the ego vehicle who monitors the ADAS system.

Instructions:

Before the Test:

1.  Participants will be asked to arrive to the Powerhouse Energy Institute parking lot with enough gasoline in their vehicles as to be able to do an hour of urban driving without needing to refill. Purchases of gasoline and other testing needs such as coffee, food, *et cetera* will be reimbursed.

2.  Because the V2V loggers require an OBD2 connection the participants’ vehicles must have a functioning OBD2 port. This will be verified before the beginning of testing, possibly be having the participant perform a short drive cycle to verify that logging is occurring. OBD2 ports should be present on all vehicles which are newer than 1996, however the function of the port may be an issue. If this cannot be solved then a participant may be asked to drive Aaron Rabinowitz’s personal vehicle, which does have a functioning OBD2 port.

3.  Once it has been determined that all participants have a functional setup, the group will drive to the Lucky’s Market parking lot on Mulberry and College.

Testing:

1.  Because the ADAS equipment requires some attention to operate there will be a pause at the Lucky’s market to make sure everything is functioning in the ego vehicle.

2.  Once all ADAS equipment is verified to be functioning, the ego vehicle driver will alert all participants to be ready to begin the test.

3.  Participants are asked to make note of the specific time when they left their parking spot to begin the test.

4.  The route described in the rout section will be driven 3 times if everything goes smoothly. Each time the route is driven, participants are asked to return to the Lucky’s Market parking lot via the Mulberry St. entrance and wait for the next cycle. Participants should attempt to stay relatively close to the ego vehicle and preferably in front; it is inevitable that the group will be separated at points, if a participant finds that he or she is very far ahead of the ego vehicle he or she should pull over and wait for the group to catch up.

Route:

The route will be

1.  Lucky’s Parking Lot

2.  West on Mulberry until Shields

3.  South on Shields until Centre

4.  Northeast on Centre until Prospect

5.  East on Prospect until College

6.  North on College until Mulberry

7.  West on Mulberry until Lucky’s Parking Lot

8.  Lucky’s Parking Lot

This route is shown in birds-eye view below, with the black arrows representing the driving directions, the black circle representing the start/end point, the red targets representing traffic signals and the blue lines representing traffic segments.
