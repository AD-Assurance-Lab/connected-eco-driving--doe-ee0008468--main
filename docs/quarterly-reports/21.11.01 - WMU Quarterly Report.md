3.  **Eco-Driving**

    1.  **Publication Status:**

Regarding the third task (Eco-Driving) we have been focusing on defining two real world driving cycles that we can utilize to test our suggested optimization method. Traffic light, distance and speed were collected for two different routes in Kalamazoo. Route (a), for a main arterial of speed limit 45 miles/hr, and the second route , route (b), for a kalamazoo downtown of a speed limit 25 miles/hr.

> <img src="media/image3.png" style="width:2.90104in;height:1.89811in" /> <img src="media/image2.png" style="width:2.67188in;height:1.87128in" />

Fig.4. (Drive cycles used for Eco-Driving study)

The optimal solution technique was implemented in matlab and used it to compute the optimal Eco-driving control for both of the above routes. Two driving cycles were used in the testing; Drive cycle 1, where the initial velocity of the Ego car is determined by the optimizer, and Drive cycle 2, where the Ego vehicle has an initial velocity half of the velocity of the lead vehicle.

> <img src="media/image7.png" style="width:4.84896in;height:3.47062in" />

FASTSim was utilized to determine the FE improvements of the suggested trajectory. The data was tested on three different vehicle types; Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs), Conventional Vehicles (CVs), and Electric Vehicles (EVs). MPGe and MPG were calculated for all the derived drive cycles, then contrasted with EPA FE measurements for city Driving.

> <img src="media/image5.png" style="width:3.40104in;height:1.81494in" />

The information mentioned above were all a part of “Autonomous Eco-Driving with Traffic Light and Lead vehicle Constraints : An Application of Best Constrained Interpolation” paper. We submitted the paper for the ECOSM conference in April, which takes place in August 2021. Right now we are waiting for the feedback of the conference and the final decision.
