1.  **Optimal EMS**

Regarding the first primary task (Optimal EMS) we have been working on evaluation of Predictive Optimal Energy Management Strategy (POEMS) applications in connected vehicles using 10 to 20-second predicted velocity for a hybrid electric vehicle (HEV). The presented methodology includes synchronous data-sets gathered in Fort Collins, Colorado using a test vehicle equipped with sensors to measure ego vehicle position and velocity, near-neighbor relative position and velocity, as well as infrastructure-derived transit time with signal phase and timing. These data-sets are utilized to compare the effect of different signal categories on prediction fidelity for different prediction horizons within a POEMS framework.

Multiple Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) algorithms use the collected data to output future vehicle velocity prediction models. These future vehicle velocity predictions are evaluated and compared using a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) metric. Additionally, the effects of different combinations of signals and different models on prediction fidelity in various prediction windows are explored. All of these combinations are ultimately addressed where the rubber meets the road: fuel economy (FE) enabled from POEMS.

The velocity predictions are implemented using limited horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) with a Dynamic Programming (DP) optimizer. This result is compared to a perfect full drive prediction using DP as well as a constant velocity within MPC. All FE results are determined in high-fidelity simulations using real-world urban arterial driving data and a validated Autonomie 2010 Toyota Prius model operating in charge sustaining mode. The full-cycle DP POEMS provides the theoretical upper limit on Fuel Economy (FE) improvement achievable with POEMS but is not currently practical for real world implementation. Perfect prediction MPC (PP-MPC) represents the upper limit of FE improvement practically achievable with POEMS but Real-Prediction MPC (RP-MPC) can provide nearly equivalent FE improvement when used with high-fidelity prediction such as those presented in this paper. Constant-Velocity MPC (CV-MPC) assumes that the vehicle will continue at the current speed for the duration of the prediction window and serves as a "null" POEMS against which other POEMS methods can be compared. Results showed that RP-MPC, enabled by high-fidelity ego future speed prediction, led to significant FE improvement over baseline nearly matching that of PP-MPC.

2.  **Eco-Driving**

We have completed an initial study in collaboration with the WMU math department and the University of Michigan addressing fuel economy improvements from systems-level data enabled through Eco-Driving. We have now demonstrated initial feasibility of the concept and are focused on a real world demonstration. This work utilizes the WMU drive-by-wire fully electric Kia Soul on our chassis dyno to evaluate custom drive cycles. We anticipate this work culminating as a journal paper with shared authorship with CSU and NREL.

1.  **Publication Status:**

Regarding the second task (Eco-Driving) we have been working on updating the manuscript and replying to the reviewers for the submitted paper on ECOSM conference with the title of “Autonomous Eco-Driving with Traffic Light and Lead vehicle Constraints : An Application of Best Constrained Interpolation”. The paper was accepted for publication for the ECOSM conference, which takes place in August 2021.
