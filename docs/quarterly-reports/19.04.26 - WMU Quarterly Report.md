**Department of Energy Project Quarterly Report**

To date, our team has helped facilitate purchase of the necessary components for data collections such as data loggers and sensors. In collaboration with Aaron from CSU, we have decided on the sensors required for our data logging as well as the drive cycle to be used for data collection. We have gathered initial radar and GPS data to confirm accuracy and calibrate the devices for a final drive cycle run. The drive cycle raw data are expected to be logged between the last week of April and first week of May.

We have also purchased and installed Argonne National Lab’s Autonomie software to begin vehicle modeling. Initial vehicle modeling has been done on MATLAB/Simulink and will be validated using Autonomie. These models will be used to evaluate vehicle energy consumption, emissions, and general performance.

Optimal EMS research has also begun by investigating implementation of artificial intelligence for velocity prediction as an input to an optimization algorithm. We are currently developing a neural network which works on comprehensive datasets gathered over numerous instances to derive improved vehicle velocity prediction. These predictions are used as input to the vehicle model, which is an Optimal EMS derivation algorithm to derive an engine torque and engine speed control strategy, that improves FE compared to current vehicle operation. On the implementation side, we are also exploring Model Predictive Control implementation.
