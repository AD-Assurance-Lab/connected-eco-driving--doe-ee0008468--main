**Energy and Mobility Improvements Project Summary**

This project began in January 2019 and is a 2-year, \$1.1 million grant from the U.S. Department of Energy Vehicle Technologies Office. It is a collaboration between Colorado State University, Western Michigan University, the National Renewable Energy Lab, the cities of Fort Collins and Denver, and the Clean Cities Coalition of Northern Colorado and Denver. The goal of the project is to derive and realize mobility and energy improvements from individual vehicle control as well as traffic control. This project leverages ongoing research within this group into the optimization of transportation infrastructure traffic management algorithms as well as vehicle level control and transportation system analysis. The interactions between the sensors and signals that enable autonomous vehicle technology and the sensors and signals that enable modern traffic management systems have not been simultaneously leveraged to achieve mobility, energy, and environmental benefits. Figure 1. Mobility data is measured using established techniques to develop mobility energy metrics. These metrics are then used to develop and evaluate an Optimal TMS and an Optimal EMS yielding mobility and energy efficiency improvements.

To date the team has gathered an initial comprehensive dataset that includes ego vehicle data, V2V, data, and various V2I data from the city of Fort Collins including traffic light signal phase and timing. The dataset was gathered using vehicles shown in Figure 2 and purchased sensors and equipment. Western Michigan is currently developing a suite of vehicle models for controls analysis, mobility metrics, velocity predictions for advanced controls, and traffic modeling.
